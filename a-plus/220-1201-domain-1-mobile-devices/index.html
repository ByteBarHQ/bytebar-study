CompTIA A+ (220-1201) Domain 1.0: Mobile Devices — Explained

Domain 1.0 covers the hardware you carry: what makes laptops different from desktops, how mobile displays and accessories work, and how these devices connect. It is the most hands-on-feeling domain of the 1101 — and the most conceptual one on this hub.

Exam: 220-1201 · weight 13% · Free study preview from ByteBar

Laptop vs. desktop: what changes

A laptop is a desktop squeezed into a thermal and power budget. That constraint drives every difference:

  • Shared components — laptop RAM (SODIMM) is physically smaller than desktop DIMMs; laptop drives historically 2.5" vs. 3.5". M.2 SSDs (NVMe or SATA) now dominate both.
  • Soldered vs. socketed — many ultrabooks solder RAM and storage to the board: nothing to upgrade, and a board failure means data-recovery difficulty. The exam cares which parts are field-replaceable.
  • Thermal design — heat pipes and tiny fans move heat from the CPU to the vents. Blocked vents cause thermal throttling: the CPU slows itself to survive, and the user experiences "random slowness."
  • Function keys and docking — Fn combos control brightness, volume, display output, and airplane mode; docking stations (or USB-C/Thunderbolt docks) turn one cable into desk setup.

Displays and what lights them

  • LCD vs. OLED — LCDs shine a backlight through liquid crystals (needs an inverter or LED driver for the backlight); OLED pixels make their own light (perfect blacks, no backlight to fail).
  • Common failures — dim but visible image: backlight/inverter failure. No image at all on laptop but fine on external monitor: panel or panel cable. Flicker or lines: cable or panel.
  • Digitizers and touch — the touch layer sits over the display; a cracked digitizer can kill touch while the picture stays perfect.
  • Resolution and aspect — it's worth knowing common pairings loosely (1080p/FHD = 1920×1080), not memorize every VESA mode.

Accessories, ports, and connectivity

  • USB generations by speed — USB 2.0 (480 Mbps), USB 3.x (5–20 Gbps), USB-C (the reversible connector that can carry USB, Thunderbolt, DisplayPort, and power). Thunderbolt 3/4 uses the USB-C connector at 40 Gbps.
  • Wireless — Wi-Fi (802.11) for LAN, Bluetooth for peripherals (pairing, short range), NFC for tap-to-pair/pay (centimeters), cellular for WWAN via SIM.
  • Synchronization — cloud sync (OneDrive, iCloud, Google) vs. local sync; the exam cares about what syncs (mail, contacts, calendars, files) and the security angle (a synced device is a data copy outside your control).
  • MDM concepts — mobile device management enrolls phones/tablets so IT can push policies, require encryption, and remote-wipe a lost device. The concept, not the console clicks.

Try it: 4 sample questions

Sample questions to check your understanding of this domain. Answers and full reasoning are tucked under each one.

1. A laptop shows a very dim image — you can just make out the desktop if you shine a flashlight on the screen. An external monitor works perfectly. What failed?

  1. A. A. The GPU
  2. B. B. The LCD panel itself
  3. C. C. The backlight or its inverter/driver
  4. D. D. The display cable
Show answer & reasoning

C. The flashlight test is decisive: if you can see the image with external light, the panel is drawing correctly — its own light source died. The external monitor working rules out the GPU (A). A dead panel (B) or cable (D) wouldn't show an image even with a flashlight.

2. A user's ultrabook has 8GB of RAM soldered to the motherboard and needs more for video editing. What is the technician's best advice?

  1. A. A. Install a SODIMM module in the empty slot
  2. B. B. Upgrade is not possible — the RAM is soldered; consider a new machine or external workflows
  3. C. C. Add a USB flash drive for ReadyBoost
  4. D. D. Replace the M.2 SSD with a larger one to add RAM
Show answer & reasoning

B. Soldered RAM cannot be upgraded — there is no slot, and desoldering isn't a field repair. The honest answer is managing expectations: optimize the workload or replace the machine. An SSD upgrade (D) adds storage, not memory; ReadyBoost (C) is a marginal cache trick, not a RAM upgrade.

3. A phone pairs with a headset over Bluetooth but will not connect to the office Wi-Fi. Which statement is true?

  1. A. A. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi use the same radio, so both must fail together
  2. B. B. They are independent radios and settings — one can fail while the other works
  3. C. C. The phone's NFC chip handles both connections
  4. D. D. Airplane mode must be on for either to work
Show answer & reasoning

B. Bluetooth (short-range peripheral pairing) and Wi-Fi (LAN) are separate radios with separate settings and separate failure modes. Troubleshooting them independently is correct — the Wi-Fi issue is likely credentials, a forgotten network, or airplane mode, while Bluetooth is proven working.

4. IT needs the ability to remote-wipe a lost company phone and enforce encryption on all mobile devices. Which approach provides this?

  1. A. A. Manually configuring each phone when it is issued
  2. B. B. Enrolling devices in a mobile device management (MDM) solution
  3. C. C. Requiring longer passcodes in the employee handbook
  4. D. D. Disabling Bluetooth on all devices
Show answer & reasoning

B. MDM enrollment gives IT centralized control: policy enforcement (encryption required), remote wipe, remote lock, and app management — at scale. Manual config (A) doesn't scale and can't wipe a lost device; a handbook rule (C) is unenforceable without the tooling.
