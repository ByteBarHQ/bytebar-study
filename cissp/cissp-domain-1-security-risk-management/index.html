CISSP Domain 1.0: Security and Risk Management — Explained

Domain 1.0 is the CISSP's worldview: security as a business function. The CIA triad, how risk is measured and treated, the models that formalize trust, and the continuity plans that keep the business alive. Think like a manager, not a technician.

Exam weight: 16% · Free study preview from ByteBar

Foundations: CIA and beyond

  • Confidentiality, integrity, availability — the triad everything serves. The CISSP adds: authenticity (it really came from who it claims), non-repudiation (they can't deny it later), and privacy (individual control over personal data).
  • Parkerian hexad — the triad's extension: adds possession/control, authenticity, and utility (encrypted data you can't decrypt has lost utility even though it's "confidential"). The exam mentions it; know the three additions.
  • DAD triad — the anti-CIA: Disclosure, Alteration, Destruction. Threats map to triad legs: disclosure hits confidentiality, alteration hits integrity, destruction hits availability.

Security models: formalizing trust

  • Bell-LaPadula — confidentiality model: "no read up, no write down." A Secret-cleared subject can't read Top Secret (no read up) and can't write to Unclassified (no write down, which would leak). Government classification systems live here.
  • Biba — integrity model: the mirror image — "no read down, no write up." Prevents low-integrity data from contaminating high-integrity processes.
  • Clark-Wilson — integrity for business: well-formed transactions, separation of duties, audit trails. The model behind "the accounting system won't let one person both create and approve a payment."
  • Least privilege, need-to-know, separation of duties — the principles the models implement. The CISSP frames every access question through these.
Bell-LaPadula = confidentiality (keep secrets from leaking down). Biba = integrity (keep garbage from flowing up). If the question mentions classified data, think Bell-LaPadula.

Risk, governance, and continuity

  • Risk management — identify, assess (likelihood × impact), treat (mitigate/transfer/accept/avoid), monitor continuously. Quantitative analysis uses SLE × ARO = ALE (single loss expectancy × annualized rate of occurrence = annualized loss expectancy).
  • Governance — policies flow from the top; the board owns security direction and accepts residual risk. Frameworks (NIST CSF, ISO 27001) give the structure; the organization supplies the decisions.
  • BCP vs. DRP — business continuity keeps the business operating (alternate sites, manual procedures); disaster recovery restores the IT. The BIA (business impact analysis) precedes both and sets RTO/RPO.
  • Personnel security — background checks, NDAs, onboarding/offboarding, mandatory vacations and job rotation (fraud needs continuous presence; forced absence exposes it).
  • Ethics and law — the CISSP code of ethics prioritizes public safety first, then clients, then the profession. Know the major regulations exist (GDPR, HIPAA, SOX) and what each protects — focus on scope, not statutes.

Try it: 4 sample questions

Sample questions to check your understanding of this domain. Answers and full reasoning are tucked under each one.

1. A defense contractor's system enforces that analysts cleared to Secret cannot read Top Secret documents. Which model is this?

  1. A. A. Biba
  2. B. B. Bell-LaPadula
  3. C. C. Clark-Wilson
  4. D. D. Brewer-Nash
Show answer & reasoning

B. Government classification + confidentiality + "no read up" = Bell-LaPadula, the confidentiality model built for exactly this. Biba (A) is the integrity mirror; Clark-Wilson (C) is commercial-transaction integrity; Brewer-Nash (D) handles conflicts of interest (consultants vs. competing clients).

2. A single loss expectancy of $100,000 with an annualized rate of occurrence of 0.5 gives what ALE, and what does it mean?

  1. A. A. $200,000 — the cost of eliminating the risk
  2. B. B. $50,000 — the expected yearly loss, used to justify control spending
  3. C. C. $100,000 — the maximum acceptable loss
  4. D. D. $50,000 — the insurance premium to buy
Show answer & reasoning

B. ALE = SLE × ARO = $100,000 × 0.5 = $50,000/year. It's the expected annual loss — the number that justifies control budgets: don't spend $200K/year to mitigate a $50K/year risk. It's a decision tool, not a premium quote (D).

3. An accounting system requires one employee to enter a payment and a different employee to approve it. Which concept is this, and which model formalizes it?

  1. A. A. Least privilege — Bell-LaPadula
  2. B. B. Separation of duties — Clark-Wilson
  3. C. C. Need to know — Biba
  4. D. D. Defense in depth — Take-Grant
Show answer & reasoning

B. Splitting a transaction across two people so neither can complete fraud alone is separation of duties, and Clark-Wilson is the integrity model that formalizes it (well-formed transactions + SoD + auditing). Least privilege (A) limits how much access one person has — related but different.

4. After a flood destroys the primary data center, the company activates its alternate site and continues serving customers while IT rebuilds systems. Which plan governed the customer-facing continuation?

  1. A. A. The disaster recovery plan (DRP)
  2. B. B. The business continuity plan (BCP)
  3. C. C. The incident response plan
  4. D. D. The risk register
Show answer & reasoning

B. Keeping the business serving customers from the alternate site is business continuity; the DRP covers restoring the technology (rebuilding systems). BCP = business keeps running; DRP = IT gets rebuilt. The scenario describes the former.
